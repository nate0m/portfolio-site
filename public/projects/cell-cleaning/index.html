Cell Cleaning

In progress
cell recoverybattery building1865021700fabrication

Oftentimes when you are harvesting li-ion cells that have been spot welded together you will find that parts of the nickel strip will be leftover on the positive and negative terminals. There are different approaches to removing these leftovers but most of them are suboptimal. Grinding them off with a dremel leaves the terminals susceptible to rust. Pulling them off with pliers always leaves a little bit leftover. Some people find it easiest to use pliers to remove all they can and move on. Which works… if you’re going to spot weld the cell again because the new nickel strips that you weld on will still make enough contact with the terminals to allow the proper current to pass through (as long as you’re not pulling too much current from each cell). I would have gone this direction if I could. But for my battery designs I have chosen not to spot weld, but to use spring contacts like you would find in your tv remote. These spring connections require even contact with the terminals otherwise they won’t be able to carry the right amount of current. This forum post is very helpful as someone did the current calculations for one of these spring contacts. Basically if the springs and the terminals point of contact is not greater than or equal to a certain amount of surface area then a large amount of resistance will build up and the battery will be in danger of damaging the cells and catching on fire.

Hence the need to remove all the leftover nickel strips from the terminals, which is called ‘cell cleaning’. But how?

With a chisel on a lever of course.
